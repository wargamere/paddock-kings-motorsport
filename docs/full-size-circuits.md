# F1 speeds and full-size circuits

The previous engine lost power linearly with speed and settled near 167 km/h. The new shared powertrain uses 900 W/kg of wheel power, a low-speed torque ceiling and eight gears. Rear tyre traction limits the launch; power and aerodynamic drag limit speed at the top end. The medium-wing standard car settles near 323 km/h on an unrestricted level straight. Actual acceleration and travelled distance change; the HUD still converts metres per second directly to km/h.

DRS cuts drag by 14%, a full tow cuts it by up to 25%, and ERS adds 150 W/kg while its battery lasts. Neither DRS nor slipstream supplies an extra engine force. Setup and career hardware still affect power, drag and grip. Player, AI, qualifying, medals and overtaking forecasts share the engine model. Traffic forecasts also reserve the tyre force already spent cornering. Lanes use smoothed world geometry, avoiding phantom slow corners where an offset path meets the road edge.

All 30 real circuits now use their listed physical lengths. The catalog scales the existing licensed approximate outlines, retaining half-metre share-code coordinates, bridge heights, road widths and career track IDs. These are not new surveyed models. Monza grows from about 1.56 km to 5.79 km; a controlled Chromium run on the low-wing setup reached 337 km/h and completed a clean lap. Speeds vary with car development, setup, weather, fuel, tyres, tow and ERS.

Editor paper, viewport fitting, pan/transform limits, draft bounds, runoff paint, physics bounds, ground, scenery and pit placement follow the larger layouts. Terrain resolution adapts to keep its heightfield below roughly 33,000 vertices, and scenery density keeps a bounded candidate count. Runoff grids stay below 40,000 cells; legacy paint grids and share codes still load. Full-size circuits can accommodate longer garage rows. Fuel consumption is 1.6 game kg/km, and medium tyre work thresholds are 1150–1750, preserving manageable fuel loads and roughly four-to-six-lap medium stints on typical real circuits. These are short-race gameplay scales, not real fuel or tyre specifications.

Full-size records use the `full-v1/` namespace. Existing compact-circuit best laps and ghosts remain stored under their original keys and cannot become impossible targets on the new geometry. Existing storage budgets and normal record eviction still apply. Medal author version 10 recomputes targets. Career calendars, championships, teams and earned development remain compatible.

Validation includes all real circuits at 100% AI, selected circuits at 110%, actual acceleration and eighth gear, full-grid traffic, pit queues, wet weather, paint/draft/share round trips, terrain allocation bounds, old-record separation and the real-browser speed/editor check. Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, `npm run test:scale:browser`, `npm run bench:physics` and `npm run bench:wet-race`.

Length sources: [bacinger/f1-circuits](https://github.com/bacinger/f1-circuits) (MIT; source file SHA `f9cc33f0e461ebc1a3777240bba9f5b2125ca252`), with current lengths for [Barcelona](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025/spain), [Singapore](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025/singapore), [Qatar](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025/qatar) and [Austin](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025/united-states) from Formula1.com's 2025 circuit pages. The [official Monza page](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025/italy) lists 5.793 km and straight-line speeds around 350 km/h.
